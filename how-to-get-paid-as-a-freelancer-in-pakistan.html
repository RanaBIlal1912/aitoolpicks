How to Get Paid as a Freelancer in Pakistan

This page contains affiliate links. We may earn a commission if you sign up, at no extra cost to you. Features and prices change, so check each tool's own site before paying.

Finding a client is only half the job. You also need a safe way to receive the money. Options and rules change, so this guide shows what to check, not a fixed answer. Confirm everything on each service's official site before relying on it.

Common ways freelancers receive payment

  • The marketplace itself. If you work through a platform, it usually has its own withdrawal options. Read its list of supported methods for Pakistan.
  • A payout service such as Payoneer. Many freelancers use a service like this to receive foreign currency. Check the current fees, supported countries and how withdrawal to your local bank works.
  • Direct bank transfer. Some clients can send money straight to your bank account. Ask your bank what details a foreign client needs and what it charges.
  • Other online wallets. Availability for Pakistan changes. PayPal and Wise, for example, do not work the same way in every country, so confirm whether you can receive money before telling a client to use them.

What to check before you choose

  1. Can I receive money in Pakistan with this method today? Look at the official support page.
  2. What are the total costs? Add up transfer fees, currency conversion and withdrawal charges.
  3. How long does it take? Ask before you promise a client a timeline.
  4. Is there buyer and seller protection? Platforms with escrow can protect you from non-payment.

Invoices and records

  • Send a simple invoice with your name, the client's name, a description of the work, the amount and the date.
  • Keep a spreadsheet or Notion page of every payment: date, client, amount and method.
  • Keep copies of contracts, messages and receipts.

Tax

Income from freelancing may be taxable in Pakistan. Rules and thresholds change, so talk to a qualified tax adviser or check the official tax authority, and keep your records in good order from day one. This page is general information, not tax or legal advice.

Protect yourself

  • Agree on price, deadline and payment method in writing before you start.
  • For larger jobs, ask for part of the payment up front or use milestones.
  • Never give out passwords or one-time codes for your payment accounts, even to a client.
