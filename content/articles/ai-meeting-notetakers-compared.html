A meeting notetaker either joins your call as a bot, or listens on your computer, and then turns the conversation into notes you can search. The product differences that matter are how it records, how long history lasts on the free plan and what it costs per person.

How this guide was made: it is a documentation-based overview. We read each vendor's official pages and recorded plans, prices and limits on the date shown; we have not run hands-on tests or benchmarks, so nothing here is a performance claim. Prices change, so confirm on the official page before you pay.

What to check first

  • How it records: some tools send a visible bot into the meeting, others capture audio on your device. Tell participants either way.
  • History limits: free plans often limit how many days or minutes of meetings you can keep.
  • Per-seat pricing: team plans are usually priced per person per month.

The tools, side by side

Meeting transcription

Otter.ai

Otter.ai records and transcribes meetings and conversations, with a free Basic plan and Pro, Business and Enterprise plans priced per user.

Good for: professionals, teams, students (20% off pro with .edu email). Watch out for: Basic has 300 minutes per month, 30 minutes per conversation and 3 lifetime file imports.

Plans (pricing verified, checked 30 Oct 2026): Basic: free; Pro: $16.99 per user/month monthly, $8.33 per user/month billed yearly; Business: $30 per user/month monthly, $19.99 per user/month billed yearly; Enterprise: custom pricing.

Notepad-style notes

Granola

Granola is an AI notepad that takes meeting notes and offers AI chat across meetings, with desktop, iOS, Android and Apple Watch apps.

Good for: professionals, teams. Watch out for: Basic has a limited meeting history (30 days) and no integrations or API access.

Plans (pricing verified, checked 30 Oct 2026): Basic: free; Business: $14 per user/month; Enterprise: $35 per user/month.

How to choose

  • Try the free plan in two or three real meetings before comparing price.
  • If a plan's price was not readable on the official page, our record says so; check it there rather than relying on a guess.
  • Check where recordings are stored and whether your company allows recording tools.
