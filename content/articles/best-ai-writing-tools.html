If you write blog posts, emails, product descriptions or client work, the right tool saves hours. You don't need all of them. Here is how to choose one.

Quick picks

Best all-rounder

ChatGPT

A general assistant: drafts, outlines, rewrites, brainstorming, and answers questions. Has a free tier, with paid plans for heavier use.

Good for: beginners who want one tool for many jobs. Skip if: you only need grammar fixes.

Try ChatGPT
Best for polishing

Grammarly

Checks spelling, grammar and tone as you type, in the browser and in many apps. A free plan covers the basics.

Good for: freelancers whose English needs to look professional to clients. Skip if: you want a tool that writes the draft for you.

Try Grammarly
Best for marketing copy

Jasper

Built around marketing content such as ads, product copy and campaign templates. It is paid, so it makes most sense if writing is part of your income.

Good for: people writing marketing copy regularly. Skip if: you are just starting and on a tight budget.

See Jasper

How to choose

  • Start free. Use the free plan of one tool for two weeks before you pay for anything.
  • Always edit the output. AI drafts can contain mistakes and generic phrasing. Add your own examples and check facts.
  • Pay only when it earns. Upgrade when the tool saves you time on paid work.

Our recommendation

Beginners: start with a free ChatGPT account and add Grammarly's free plan for proofreading. Consider a paid plan once you have regular clients.
