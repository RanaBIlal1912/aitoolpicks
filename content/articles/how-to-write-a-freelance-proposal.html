Most clients receive many proposals and read only the first few lines. A good proposal is short, specific and about the client's problem, not about you.

The structure that works

  1. Start with their problem. Show you read the job post in one sentence.
  2. Say how you will solve it. Two or three clear steps.
  3. Show one relevant sample. Link to one piece of work that matches the job.
  4. Be clear about price and time. Give a number and a delivery date, or say what you need to know to give one.
  5. End with one easy question. It makes replying simple.

A template to edit

Hi [Name],

I read your post about [their problem]. I can help by [step one], [step two] and [step three].

Here is a similar piece of work: [link].

I can deliver by [date] for [price]. Do you want the final files as [format A] or [format B]?

Thanks,
[Your name]

Replace every bracket with real details. A template is a starting point, not something to send unchanged. Clients notice copy-pasted messages.

Common mistakes

  • Long paragraphs about your life story.
  • Promising results you cannot control.
  • Sending the same message to every job.
  • Spelling mistakes in the first line.

Before and after

Weak opening:

Hello sir, I am a hard-working freelancer with many years of experience and I can do your job at a cheap price.

Stronger opening:

Hi Sara, you need product descriptions for 40 new items before your spring launch. I can write them in your friendly brand tone and send the first five tomorrow so you can check the style.

The second version shows you read the job, names a concrete next step and removes risk for the client. It does not need big claims.

A quick quality check

  • Does the first line mention something specific from the job post?
  • Is there one sample that matches the job?
  • Did you give a price and a date, or ask the one question you need to give them?
  • Is it short enough to read on a phone?

Following up

If you hear nothing, one polite follow-up after several days is reasonable. Add something useful, such as a relevant idea or a small question, rather than just asking if they saw your message. If there is still no reply, move on and keep the contact for later.

Pricing in a proposal

  • Say what is included and what is not, so extras do not become arguments.
  • For a first job with a new client, consider a small scoped project so both sides can see how you work.
  • Do not undercut yourself so far that you resent the work.
