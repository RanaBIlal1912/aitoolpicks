Freelancing works the same way almost everywhere: pick a skill people pay for, prove you can do it, find a few clients, deliver well and get paid. This plan breaks that into four weeks you can follow from any country, with free tools first.

Week 1: choose one service and learn it properly

  • Pick one service for one type of client. "Product descriptions for small online shops" is easier to sell than "writing".
  • Check there is demand. Search job boards and marketplaces for the service and read what clients actually ask for.
  • Finish one focused course or project. A small, complete piece of work teaches more than ten half-watched tutorials.

Week 2: build proof

Clients trust evidence. Make three samples that match the service, and be honest about which ones are practice projects. Put them in a simple portfolio; the guide to building a free portfolio website shows how.

Week 3: set up profiles and payment

  1. Create one marketplace profile and write it around the client's result, not your life story.
  2. Add a clear photo or logo, your service, your three samples and how fast you reply.
  3. Decide how you will be paid before you need to be. Check which payout methods your marketplace and your country support, and what each costs; see how freelancers get paid.

Week 4: reach out every day

Send a few short, personal messages a day to businesses you could help, and apply to a small number of well-matched jobs. The structure in our proposal guide keeps messages brief, and getting your first client covers where to look.

Tools that help, without spending

  • A free AI assistant for outlines and first drafts, with every output checked by you.
  • A proofreader for client messages.
  • A free notes or database tool to track clients and deadlines.

Keep yourself safe

  • Never pay a fee to receive work. Real clients pay you.
  • Be cautious with anyone who wants to move you off a platform's protected payments.
  • Agree scope, price and deadline in writing, and never share passwords or one-time codes.
  • Keep records of income. Tax rules differ by country, so check with your local tax authority or a qualified adviser.
