"AI coding tool" covers three quite different things: assistants that sit inside your editor, agents that take a task and work on it, and builders that turn a prompt into a working app. Picking the wrong type is the most common reason people feel a tool is not worth the money.

How this guide was made: it is a documentation-based overview. We read each vendor's official pages and recorded plans, prices and limits on the date shown; we have not run hands-on tests or benchmarks, so nothing here is a performance claim. Prices change, so confirm on the official page before you pay.

Three kinds of tool

  • Editor assistants complete and explain code while you work in your own project (GitHub Copilot, Cursor).
  • Coding agents are given a task and work through it with less supervision (Devin).
  • App and website builders generate a whole app from a description, mostly for prototypes and non-developers (Replit, Lovable, Bolt, v0).

The tools, side by side

Editor assistant

GitHub Copilot

GitHub Copilot is an AI coding assistant with inline suggestions, chat, an agent mode and a command-line tool, sold in Free, Pro, Pro+ and Max plans plus business offerings.

Good for: individual developers, teams (business and enterprise offerings). Watch out for: Free plan is limited to 2,000 inline suggestions per month and entry-level models.

Plans (pricing verified, checked 30 Oct 2026): Free: free; Pro: $10/month; Pro+: $39/month; Max: $100/month.

Editor assistant

Cursor

Cursor is an AI-assisted code editor. Its pricing page lists Free, Plus, Premium, Super and Ultra individual plans plus Teams and Enterprise.

Good for: individual developers, teams (teams and enterprise plans). Watch out for: Third-party model usage is billed separately from the plan price.

Plans (pricing partly verified, checked 30 Oct 2026): Free: free; Plus: $8/month; Premium: $30/month; Super: $100/month; Ultra: $200/month.

Devin is Cognition's AI coding product. The old windsurf.com/pricing address now redirects to devin.ai/pricing, which lists Free, Pro, Max, Teams and Enterprise plans.

Good for: individual developers, teams of up to 200 users. Watch out for: Exact quota figures are not stated.

Plans (pricing verified, checked 30 Oct 2026): Free: free; Pro: $20/month; Max: $200/month; Teams: $40 per user/month; Enterprise: custom pricing.

Replit

Replit is an online coding environment with an AI agent for building and deploying apps. Its pricing page lists Core, Pro and Enterprise plans.

Good for: individuals, small teams (pro, up to 15 collaborators). Watch out for: The page we read did not show a separate free plan; 'Free Mode' limits appear under Core.

Plans (pricing partly verified, checked 30 Oct 2026): Core: $20/month monthly, $18/month billed yearly; Pro: $100/month monthly, $90/month billed yearly; Enterprise: custom pricing.

Good for: non-developers, founders and small teams. Watch out for: Credits expire (monthly plan credits after 2 months).

How to choose

  • You write code every day: start with an editor assistant and use its free plan on a real project for two weeks.
  • You have well-defined chores to hand off: an agent may fit, but check how usage is metered before you commit.
  • You need a working prototype and do not code: try a builder, and read what happens to the code and hosting when you stop paying.
  • You work in a team: compare the team plans, not the individual ones; they differ in price, admin controls and data terms.

Before you pay

  • Check how each plan meters usage (requests, credits or an allowance) and what happens when you reach it.
  • Check the data terms before using AI on a client's or employer's private code.
  • Where our record says pricing is only partly verified, treat the numbers as indicative and open the official page.
