Research tools do not all do the same job. Some search papers, some show how a paper has been cited, some let you ask questions of documents you upload, and some answer from the open web with links. Mixing them up is the usual reason a tool disappoints.

How this guide was made: it is a documentation-based overview. We read each vendor's official pages and recorded plans, prices and limits on the date shown; we have not run hands-on tests or benchmarks, so nothing here is a performance claim. Prices change, so confirm on the official page before you pay.

Four jobs

  • Find papers: academic search tools such as Elicit and Consensus.
  • Check how a paper is cited: Scite's Smart Citations show whether later studies support or contradict a claim.
  • Question your own sources: Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) works from documents you give it.
  • Answer from the web with links: Perplexity.

A rule that always applies

Open the sources. An AI summary can misread a paper, so treat it as a pointer to what to read, never as the evidence itself, and cite the original.

The tools, side by side

Paper search

Elicit

Elicit is an AI research assistant for finding and analysing academic papers, with a free Basic plan and Pro, Scale and Enterprise plans.

Good for: researchers, students, institutions. Watch out for: Free usage of the Research Agent and Reports is limited.

Plans (pricing verified, checked 30 Oct 2026): Basic: free; Pro: $49 per user/month billed yearly; Scale: $229 per user/month billed yearly; Enterprise: custom pricing.

Paper search

Consensus

Consensus is an AI research tool for finding answers in scientific papers. Its pricing page lists Individual plus Team and Enterprise options.

Good for: researchers, students. Watch out for: The pricing page we read showed Monthly and Annual toggles but no prices.

Plans (pricing partly verified, checked 30 Oct 2026): Individual: price not captured (see the tool page); Team and Enterprise: custom pricing.

Citation context

Scite

Scite is an AI-powered platform that helps researchers discover and evaluate scientific literature, using Smart Citations to show whether studies support or contradict a claim.

Good for: researchers, students, institutions. Watch out for: No free plan was stated on the pricing page.

Plans (pricing verified, checked 30 Oct 2026): Basic: $12/month billed yearly; Pro: $40/month billed yearly; Enterprise: custom pricing.

Visit Scite
Your own sources

Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM)

Gemini Notebook, which this catalog listed as NotebookLM until Google renamed it, is Google's notebook tool for working with your own sources. Google's support page describes free and paid tiers by notebook and source limits.

Good for: students, researchers, professionals. Watch out for: Free tier: 100 notebooks per user and 50 sources per notebook.

Plans (pricing partly verified, checked 30 Oct 2026): Standard (free): free; Google AI Plus: price not captured (see the tool page); Google AI Pro: price not captured (see the tool page); Google AI Ultra (20 TB / 30 TB): price not captured (see the tool page).

Web answers with links

Perplexity

Perplexity is an AI answer engine that searches the web and shows the sources behind an answer, so you can open and check them.

Good for: researchers, students, professionals who need cited answers. Watch out for: Free usage is limited.

Plans (pricing verified, checked 30 Oct 2026): Free: free; Pro: $17/month billed yearly; Max: $167/month billed yearly; Enterprise: custom pricing.

Visit Perplexity

How to choose

  • Start from the job above, then check the free plan against your real workload.
  • Compare how each tool shows its sources; you should be able to click through to the original paper every time.
  • Check whether your institution allows uploading its documents to a third-party service.
