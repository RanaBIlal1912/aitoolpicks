AI tools can make studying faster, but they can also get you in trouble if you use them the wrong way. This guide covers a few useful tools and a simple rule: use them to understand and check your work, not to hand in work you did not do.

Before you start: know your school's rules

Every school and teacher has a different policy on AI. Some allow it for brainstorming, some ban it completely. Read your course rules, and if you are unsure, ask your teacher. Never submit text written by an AI as your own work.

Understand

ChatGPT

Ask it to explain a hard idea in simpler words, give examples, or quiz you on a topic. It has a free tier and paid plans for heavier use.

Watch out: it can be wrong and sound sure about it. Check facts in your textbook or class notes.

Try ChatGPT
Check

Grammarly

Catches spelling and grammar mistakes in essays and emails to teachers. A free plan is available.

Watch out: suggestions are not always right. You decide what stays, and it does not replace reading your work aloud.

Try Grammarly
Organise

Notion

Keep class notes, assignment deadlines and a reading list in one place. A free plan is available for personal use.

Watch out: a complicated setup wastes more time than it saves. Start with one page per subject.

Try Notion
Present

Canva

Build clean slides and posters from templates for class presentations. It has a free plan.

Watch out: pretty slides do not fix weak content. Spend your time on the message first.

Try Canva

A simple honest-use routine

  1. Write your own notes and first draft.
  2. Ask an AI assistant to explain what you did not understand, then close it and rewrite in your own words.
  3. Proofread with a grammar tool and fix mistakes yourself.
  4. Keep a note of how you used any tool, in case your teacher asks.
