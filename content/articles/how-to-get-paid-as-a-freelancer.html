Winning a client is only half the job. Getting paid safely, on time and without surprise fees depends on a few decisions you can make before you start work. Rules and available services differ from country to country, so this guide focuses on what to check rather than a fixed answer.

Ways freelancers are paid

  • Through a marketplace. The platform holds payment and releases it to you. Read its list of supported withdrawal methods for your country.
  • Bank transfer. Direct, but it can involve intermediary fees and exchange-rate margins when currencies differ.
  • Payment services and wallets. Availability, fees and receiving limits depend on the country you live in, so confirm on the provider's own site that you can receive money before promising it to a client.
  • Invoicing tools. Useful for repeat clients, with a record of every invoice.

Questions to answer before you choose

  1. Can I receive money with this method in my country today?
  2. What are the total costs: transfer fee, currency conversion and withdrawal charges?
  3. How long does a payment take to arrive?
  4. Is there protection if a client does not pay?

An invoice checklist

  • Your details and the client's, with a unique invoice number and date.
  • A clear description of the work and the period it covers.
  • The amount, currency and any tax that applies.
  • How to pay and the date payment is due.

Protect your cash flow

For larger jobs, split the work into milestones and agree what triggers each payment, such as part before you start and the rest on delivery. Put it in writing. Send invoices promptly and follow up politely when one is late.

Signs of a payment scam

  • A client overpays and asks you to refund the difference.
  • You are asked to pay a fee or buy something before you can start.
  • You are pushed to leave the platform and its protected payments.
  • Anyone asks for passwords, verification codes or banking logins.

Records and tax

Keep a simple log of every payment: date, client, amount, currency and method, plus invoices and receipts. Freelance income can be taxable, and the rules depend on where you live, so check with the official tax authority or a qualified adviser. This page is general information, not tax or legal advice.
