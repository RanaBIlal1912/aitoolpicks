A portfolio, blog or small shop needs a domain name and hosting. Beginners usually overpay because of low first-year prices that jump at renewal.

What to check before you buy

  • Renewal price, not just the intro price.
  • Free SSL (the padlock in the browser) should be included.
  • Easy setup: one-click WordPress or a simple site builder.
  • Support: live chat is helpful when you are new.
  • Backups and a refund window.

Do you even need hosting yet?

If you only want a simple portfolio, a free site builder or a free static host can be enough to start. Pay for hosting once you need a custom domain email, a blog, or a shop.

A checklist to compare hosts

  • Price now and at renewal. Introductory prices can differ from the price after the first term.
  • Refund policy. How long can you cancel and get money back?
  • Support. Is help available when you are stuck, and in which channels?
  • Backups. Are backups included, and can you restore one yourself?
  • Security basics. Is an SSL certificate included?
  • Room to grow. Can you upgrade without moving your site?

Do you need paid hosting at all?

If you only need a simple portfolio, a free static host can be enough. Paid hosting makes sense when you want your own domain, a business email or a site you can edit with a builder or a content system. Be honest about what you need today, not what you might need in three years.

After you buy: your first five tasks

  1. Turn on two-step login for your account.
  2. Point your domain to the host and wait for it to update.
  3. Install the site tool you chose, or start the builder.
  4. Check that the SSL padlock shows on every page.
  5. Set a reminder for the renewal date so a price change does not surprise you.
